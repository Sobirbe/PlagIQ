# Uzbek AI-written text: measurement before building (November 2026)

**Question:** can PlagIQ tell Uzbek academic text written or polished by an AI chatbot from human-written Uzbek text, with
very few false alarms on human writing? No detector has been shown to work on Uzbek (Pangram does not support it).
**Status:** measurement only. Nothing here is used by the website or the engine.

## Data (folder `evaluation/ai_uz/`)
- **Human:** 493 Uzbek academic abstracts published 2010–2021 (before ChatGPT), from OpenAlex, language confirmed by
  PlagIQ's detector, including Uzbek works OpenAlex labels Russian or English (`fetch_human.py`, which re-creates
  `human_uz.json`; the texts are other authors' and are not kept in the repository).
- **AI-written:** 20 Uzbek abstracts written by Claude (an Anthropic model) for the titles of 20 human abstracts
  (`ai_batch1.json`, same topics, so topic cannot give the answer away).
- **AI-polished:** 16 human abstracts rewritten by Claude "in better academic style" (`ai_polished1.json`).
- **ChatGPT:** two independent runs of the prompts in `chatgpt-prompts.txt`, pasted by the owner from ChatGPT:
  run 1 (`chatgpt.json`: 50 written for titles, 15 polished) and run 2 (`chatgpt2.json`: 60 written, 15 polished; the
  same titles and originals, different answers), plus a third answer to block A1 (`chatgpt3.json`, 10 written).
- **Gemini:** 6 complete synthetic articles (economics, computer science, ecology, medicine, pedagogy, linguistics)
  generated by the owner with Gemini (`gemini1.txt`), and 6 more that Gemini was asked to make read as human-written
  ("humanized", `gemini2_humanized.txt`); their numbers and references are invented by the model.
- **ChatGPT, full length:** one complete synthetic PhD dissertation in Uzbek with Russian passages, generated by the owner
  with ChatGPT (`chatgpt_dissertation.txt`); its statistics and "implementation certificates" are invented.
- **Gemini abstracts:** Gemini's answers to the same 9 blocks (`gemini.json`: 60 written, 15 polished), pasted by the owner.
- **Human full texts:** 250 open-access Uzbek works 2010–2021 (139 Zenodo, 111 inscience.uz), each with a CC BY licence
  confirmed on its own page and fetched as robots.txt allows (`fetch_fulltext.py`); 2,749 Uzbek passages of 120–260
  words, about 369,000 words. Not kept in the repository.
- **Full-length chatbot answers:** Gemini's, Claude's and ChatGPT's answers to `prompts-full-uz.txt`
  (`full_gemini.json`, `full_claude.json`, `full_chatgpt.json`): per chatbot 24 sections, 12 polished and 6 continued
  passages of the human full texts above.
- **Planned:** recent human-written student texts (the false-alarm test that decides any use).

## First feasibility check (`baseline.py`)
Text is put into one script (Cyrillic → Latin), apostrophes unified and spacing/quotation quirks removed first, and
Uzbek abstracts that include a Russian version are left out, so the classifier cannot use alphabet or formatting.
Character n-grams + logistic regression, 5-fold cross-validation; polished texts and their human originals are only
test data.

| Threshold set for false alarms on human abstracts | AI-written caught | AI-polished caught | Human originals of the polished texts flagged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 % | 45 % | 19 % | 0 % |
| 5 % | 100 % | 38 % | 6 % |

AI-written abstracts use stock phrases far more (e.g. "…tahlil qilinadi" in 90 % vs 18 % of human abstracts,
"…ko‘rib chiqilgan" 85 % vs 11 %) and shorter, more uniform sentences.

## Second check: does it recognise another chatbot? (`crossmodel.py`)
ChatGPT's answers to block A1 (10 abstracts, unedited, `chatgpt.json`) were scored by a classifier trained only on human
and Claude-written abstracts, with thresholds set on held-out human abstracts:

| Threshold set for false alarms on human abstracts | Claude-written (held out) caught | ChatGPT-written (never seen) caught |
|---|---|---|
| 1 % | 45 % | **0 %** |
| 5 % | 100 % | **20 %** |

A detector trained on one model's Uzbek does **not** recognise another model's: ChatGPT uses other stock openings and
verbs ("Maqolada …", "bayon etiladi", "tavsiflanadi"). Every chatbot students use must be in the training data.

## Third check: train on several chatbots (`combined.py`)
Every text is grouped with its topic (a human abstract, the AI abstracts written from its title, the AI polishes of it)
and 5-fold cross-validation splits by group, so no test topic is seen in training. The human originals of polished texts
are never trained on. Human abstracts: 381 (+31 polished originals); thresholds set on held-out human abstracts.
Percentages are AI texts caught at the threshold where 1 % (5 %) of human abstracts are flagged.

| Trained on human + … | Claude written (20) | ChatGPT written (120, 3 runs) | Gemini written (60) | Polished: Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini (16 / 30 / 15) | Polished originals flagged (31) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude | 70 % (90 %) | 12–42 % (30–63 %) | 27 % (48 %) | 25 / 13–20 / 7 % | 6 % (10 %) |
| Claude + ChatGPT | 75 % (95 %) | **98–100 %** (100 %) | 55 % (92 %) | 25 / 13–20 / 7 % | 3 % (10 %) |
| Claude + ChatGPT + Gemini | 55 % (85 %) | 80–100 % (100 %) | **92 %** (100 %) | 19 / 7 / 7 % | 0 % (10 %) |
| … + all polished | 45 % (75 %) | 76–85 % (100 %) | 75 % (100 %) | 25 / 7–13 / 0 % | 0 % (6 %) |

With ~4 human texts above a 1 % threshold and 10–20 texts in a group, single texts move results by 5–10 points.

- **Abstracts written from scratch:** a chatbot in the training data is caught almost always (92–100 % at 1 % false
  alarms). An unseen one is caught partly: Gemini 55 % by the Claude + ChatGPT detector, Claude-only detectors catch
  12–42 % of ChatGPT. One simple classifier for all chatbots gets less sharp at 1 % as chatbots are added (Claude 75 → 55 %).
- **Polished:** not solved. Polished texts are caught at about the rate the human originals themselves are flagged;
  adding polished examples to training does not help. Polishing keeps the human author's content and much of the
  wording, and a style classifier on 61 examples cannot separate it.
- **Gemini slips:** Gemini's polish of Cyrillic originals left words half in Cyrillic inside Latin text ("эндометриоз",
  "ножўя (nojo‘ya)", "uстувор"); the ChatGPT dissertation switched into Kazakh mid-sentence. Possible cheap integrity
  signals, to be counted on more data before any use.

## Fourth check: full-length texts (`score_articles.py`)
The 34 prose paragraphs of the 6 Gemini articles (40+ words each; headings, formulas and references dropped) were scored
by detectors trained on abstracts only, against thresholds from held-out human abstracts.

| Trained on human + … | Gemini paragraphs flagged at 1 % false alarms | at 5 % |
|---|---|---|
| Claude written | 6 % (2 of 34) | 18 % (6 of 34) |
| Claude + ChatGPT written | 3 % (1 of 34) | 12 % (4 of 34) |
| Claude + ChatGPT + Gemini written | 0 % | 15 % (5 of 34) |
| *"Humanized" Gemini articles (35 paragraphs):* | | |
| Claude written | 0 % | 9 % (3 of 35) |
| Claude + ChatGPT written | 0 % | 0 % |
| Claude + ChatGPT + Gemini written | 0 % | 0 % |
| *ChatGPT PhD dissertation (24 Uzbek paragraphs; Russian sentences and tables dropped):* | | |
| Claude written | 0 % | 4 % (1 of 24) |
| Claude + ChatGPT written | 0 % | 0 % |
| Claude + ChatGPT + Gemini written | 0 % | 0 % |

Full-length text is essentially **not recognised**, even from a chatbot whose abstracts are in training: the ChatGPT
dissertation is missed by the detector trained on 120 ChatGPT abstracts, and the Gemini articles by the one trained on
60 Gemini abstracts. What a detector learns is a chatbot's *abstract* habits ("Maqolada … yoritilgan"), not its
Uzbek in general; genre matters more than the chatbot. The training data must cover each chatbot **and** each genre that
will be checked (dissertation chapters, articles, essays), not only abstracts.

**What this does not show:** AI texts are still few (20 Claude-written, 120 ChatGPT-written, 60 Gemini-written, 61 polished); a classifier may learn one model's habits
rather than "AI"; human OpenAlex abstracts carry their own quirks (typos, run-on sentences) that student documents may
not; abstracts are one short, formulaic genre. Polished text is the hard case and the most common student use. A detector good on written-from-scratch text could still be detecting "clean recent text" vs older, untidier human abstracts; it must be tested on recent human-written student work before any use. No figure here may be used to
describe a product.

## Fifth check: XLM-R (`xlmr_study.py`)
XLM-R base (FacebookAI/xlm-roberta-base, MIT licence, revision e73636d4) on the same Uzbek abstracts, topic-grouped folds
and human-set thresholds as the third check, trained on human + all AI texts (written and polished: regime E4). On this
machine's CPU the full model could not be fine-tuned in time, so the lower 6 of its 12 layers stay fixed and the top 6
plus the classifier are trained (5 epochs, lr 1e-4, inputs cut at 192 tokens; about 15 minutes per fold).

- **XLM-R as a fixed encoder** + logistic regression is worse than the char n-gram detector (e.g. ChatGPT 0–73 %,
  Claude 25 % at 1 % false alarms): its general-purpose representation does not capture style.
- **Fine-tuned**, it ranks texts about as well as char n-grams fold by fold (AUC 0.876–0.972, mean 0.940, against
  0.882–0.975, mean 0.933), but at the strict end it is worse: in every fold a few human abstracts get near-certain AI
  scores (0.98–0.99, the AI median), which pushes the 1 % threshold up.
- **Combined** (the two scores averaged as ranks within each fold) it is better than either:

| At 1 % false alarms (5 %) | char n-gram | XLM-R fine-tuned | combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude written (20) | 45 % (75 %) | 30 % (65 %) | **60 %** (90 %) |
| ChatGPT written (3 runs) | 76–85 % (100 %) | 10–43 % (70–82 %) | **80–97 %** (98–100 %) |
| Gemini written (60) | 75 % (100 %) | 37 % (80 %) | **93 %** (98 %) |
| Polished, Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini | 25 / 7–13 / 0 % | 12 / 0 / 0 % | 25 / 20 / 13 % |
| Polished originals flagged (31) | 0 % (6 %) | 0 % (6 %) | 0 % (10 %) |

- **Full length is unchanged:** fold models trained on abstracts flag none of the Gemini article or ChatGPT dissertation
  paragraphs at 1 % (at 5 %: 1 of 93) — and none of 2,381–2,749 human full-text passages either (1 at 5 %). Long text
  scores low whatever its source; full-length AI examples must be in training.
- So a stronger model adds a modest gain on abstracts and nothing on polished or full-length text: the limit is the
  training data (genres, chatbots, polishing), not the model.

## Sixth check: full-length text (`full_study.py`)
Human side: 2,731 passages (120–260 words) of the 250 open-access works. AI side: answers to `prompts-full-uz.txt`
from Gemini, Claude and ChatGPT (`full_gemini.json`, `full_claude.json`, `full_chatgpt.json`; chatbots as confirmed by the
owner — the Claude set was first given as ChatGPT and corrected) — per chatbot 24 article sections written for titles of
those human works (blocks C), 12 polished human passages (D) and 6 continuations of human passages (E) — plus the earlier
Gemini articles, "humanized" Gemini articles and ChatGPT dissertation, their paragraphs merged into pieces of at least
120 words. Every text is grouped with its source work and 5-fold cross-validation splits by group; polished, partly-AI
(human passage + continuation) and the human originals of D/E are test-only unless stated. Char n-gram detector;
thresholds from held-out human passages.

**Length matters here:** Claude's and ChatGPT's sections have a median of about 310 words, Gemini's 230, the human
passages 131. At full length Claude's sections were caught 23–24 of 24 *even when Claude was left out of training* —
mostly a length effect. The table therefore gives the run with **every text cut to its first 120 words** (partly-AI texts,
whose continuation the cut removes, are caught 0 of 6 that way; at full length 0–4 of 6). Counts: caught at 1 % (5 %) of
held-out human passages flagged.

| Trained on human passages + writing by … | Gemini sections (24) | Claude sections (24) | ChatGPT sections (24) | Gemini articles (17) | "Humanized" Gemini (18) | ChatGPT dissertation (12) | Polished: Gemini / Claude / ChatGPT (12 each) | Continuation alone: Gemini / Claude / ChatGPT (6 each) | Human originals of D/E (18) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all three chatbots | 22 (23) | 20 (23) | 19 (23) | 13 (16) | 9 (15) | 0 (3) | 1 / 0 / 3 | 3 / 2 / 2 | 0 (2) |
| Claude left out | 22 (24) | **13 (20)** | 18 (23) | 13 (16) | 11 (13) | 0 (3) | 4 / 0 / 3 | 3 / 1 / 3 | 0 (2) |
| Gemini left out | **13 (18)** | 19 (23) | 20 (24) | 6 (12) | 3 (6) | 0 (0) | 0 / 0 / 2 | 1 / 2 / 3 | 0 (1) |
| ChatGPT left out | 22 (24) | 18 (23) | **15 (20)** | 14 (16) | 9 (16) | 0 (6) | 2 / 0 / 1 | 3 / 2 / 0 | 0 (2) |
| all + polished in training | 23 (24) | 16 (22) | 19 (24) | 13 (15) | 9 (14) | 0 (1) | 3 / 1 / 4 | 1 / 2 / 3 | 0 (2) |

- **A chatbot in training is caught in full-length text**, also at equal length: 19–22 of 24 sections at 1 %. With
  abstracts-only training the same kinds of text were caught 0 of 93.
- **An unseen chatbot is caught about half the time** (13–15 of 24 at 1 %, 18–20 at 5 %) when the two others are in
  training — more than with one other chatbot (7–11 of 24 at 1 %, Gemini and Claude only). More chatbots in training help
  with a new one, but every chatbot students use should still be in training.
- **The ChatGPT dissertation is never caught at 1 %** (0 of 12), although ChatGPT's sections are (19–20 of 24): it came
  from a different chat and genre (a whole dissertation, mixed with Russian). One chatbot's output varies a lot; more
  genres per chatbot are needed.
- **Polished** passages stay out of reach (0–4 of 12 at 1 %), also with polished examples in training.
- **Partly-AI** documents scored as one text are caught 0–4 of 6 (full length), and the continuations alone 0–3 of 6.
  Partly-AI work has to be scored passage by passage, highlighting the AI part.
- **False alarms on clean human writing:** the 18 human originals of D/E (picked for clean, well-formed text) are flagged
  0 of 18 at the 1 % threshold; recent careful student work still has to be measured before any use.
- Numbers are small (6–24 AI texts per group); one text moves a result by 4–17 points.

## On the website: the experimental indicator (`uzai.py`, `train_uzai.py`)
`evaluation/ai_uz/train_uzai.py` trains one model on everything above except polished texts (human, all written before
ChatGPT: 412 abstracts, 2,749 passages of 2010–21 works and 2,992 passages of 2021–22 journal issues; chatbot: 337 abstracts, sections, continuations, articles and the dissertation) with `uzai.features`, the
code the website runs, and writes `models/uz_ai_v1.npz` (1.2 MB; numpy only at run time). The website scores a document
passage by passage (120–260 words, sentence-aligned; Uzbek passages only) and marks passages above one threshold.

**The threshold.** Abstracts score higher than other human writing: at the threshold where 1 % of held-out human passages
are flagged, 21 % of held-out human abstracts would be. A student's abstract is scored like any passage, so the threshold
is the higher one — 1 % of human passages *and* of human abstracts (0.880). Cross-validated (grouped as above), at it:

| Held out | Caught |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT abstracts (3 runs) / sections | 75 of 120 / 19 of 24 |
| Gemini abstracts / sections / articles / "humanized" | 33 of 60 / 14 of 24 / 3 of 17 / 0 of 18 |
| Claude abstracts / sections | 11 of 20 / 5 of 24 |
| Continuations alone (all three) / ChatGPT dissertation | 0 of 18 / 0 of 12 |
| **Human passages / human abstracts wrongly flagged** | **0 of 2,749 / 5 of 412** |

So the website indicator is deliberately cautious: few false alarms on 2010–2021 human writing, at the cost of missing
much chatbot text (most of Claude's, nearly all polished or continued text). A result without flags does not mean
human-written; a flag is a reason to look closer, not proof.

**Who sees it.** `UZ_AI_DETECT` = `off` | `preview` (default) | `on`. In preview only site owners and accounts with a pilot
allowance get it. It stays in preview until its false-alarm rate on recent (2023+) human student writing has been
measured. The result is shown on the page only: never cached, stored, put in the PDF or on the certificate.

**Recent journals, and why only pre-ChatGPT text is trained on as human.** The owner supplied, or gave permission to
use, *Agrobiznes, fan va texnologiyalar* 2023 No. 2, all 25 issues of IMRS's *Iqtisodiyot: tahlillar va prognozlar*
2021–2026 (imrs.uz) and 20 issues of *Management, Marketing and Finance* 2024–2026 (jmmf.uz); `build_recent.py` turns
them into 8,886 Uzbek passages (texts not in the repository; robots.txt allowed both sites). The owner reports that most
Uzbek publications since ChatGPT (30 November 2022) are partly AI-generated, so **only issues published before it
(IMRS 2021–2022: 2,992 passages) are trained on as human**; later issues are scored only, by year.

Training on 2023–2026 journals as human had cost detection — the model learned chatbot-assisted writing as human
(e.g. Claude sections 5 of 24 caught, ChatGPT 17). With the pre-ChatGPT rule, cross-validated (grouped as above):

| | Caught / flagged |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT abstracts (3 runs) / sections | 90 of 120 / 20 of 24 |
| Gemini abstracts / sections / articles / "humanized" | 41 of 60 / 15 of 24 / 1 of 17 / 0 of 18 |
| Claude abstracts / sections | 12 of 20 / 11 of 24 |
| Continuations alone (6 per chatbot) / ChatGPT dissertation | 2 of 18 / 0 of 12 |
| **Human passages / of which journal 2021–22 / human abstracts, wrongly flagged** | **0 of 5,741 / 0 of 2,992 / 5 of 412** |

Threshold 0.875. 2023+ journal passages (never trained on) flagged: IMRS 0 of 967 (2023), 0 of 625 (2024), 0 of 490
(2025), 1 of 349 (2026); Agrobiznes 0 of 132 (2023); JMMF 1 of 473 (2024), 4 of 1,535 (2025), **36 of 1,323 (2026)**.
The indicator flags only text very close to the chatbot writing it learned, so these are lower bounds on chatbot use,
not estimates of it.

**Prompt files with others' abstracts.** `chatgpt-prompts.txt`, `gemini-prompts.txt` and `prompts-ru.txt` (on the
`development/uz-ai-study` branch) quote human abstracts from OpenAlex whose licences were not checked, so they are not
on the main branch; `prompts-full-uz.txt` quotes only passages whose CC BY licence was confirmed, with their sources.

## Russian (`ru_study.py`, started November 2026)
The site checks Russian as well as Uzbek (Russian is currently sent to Pangram). Same method: 780 Russian abstracts
2010–2021 by authors at Uzbek institutions (`fetch_human_ru.py`, not kept in the repository) against chatbot answers to
`prompts-ru.txt` (60 titles to write for, 15 abstracts to polish; `ru_plan.json`), saved as `ru_<chatbot>.json`.
LaTeX, Markdown, quote/dash variants and ё are removed first.

Percentages: AI texts caught at the threshold where 1 % (5 %) of held-out human abstracts are flagged (60 written
and 15 polished per chatbot; 15 polished originals).

| Trained on human + written by … | ChatGPT written | Claude written | Gemini written | Polished (ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini) | Polished originals flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all three | **100 %** (100 %) | 53 % (80 %) | 57 % (78 %) | 7 / 0 / 7 % | 0 % (0 %) |
| Claude + Gemini (ChatGPT unseen) | 35 % (67 %) | 50 % (73 %) | 63 % (80 %) | 7 / 0 / 13 % | 0 % (7 %) |
| ChatGPT + Gemini (Claude unseen) | 100 % (100 %) | 27 % (53 %) | 62 % (80 %) | 0 / 0 / 7 % | 0 % (0 %) |
| ChatGPT + Claude (Gemini unseen) | 100 % (100 %) | 57 % (88 %) | 20 % (60 %) | 13 / 0 / 7 % | 0 % (0 %) |

- ChatGPT's Russian is very recognisable once it is in training (100 %), and not because of its padding: 44 of its 60
  abstracts end with one of four identical stock sentences ("Дополнительно обозначены вопросы, требующие более
  подробной эмпирической проверки." etc.), but with those sentences removed it is still caught 100 % (40 % unseen).
- Claude's and Gemini's Russian is caught only about half the time even when in training: Russian academic prose is
  formulaic in humans too, so the styles overlap more than in Uzbek.
- An unseen chatbot is mostly missed (20–35 % at 1 %); polished text is not caught (0–13 %).
- ChatGPT wrote one word with a Latin letter inside Cyrillic ("многoантенных"), the same kind of slip seen in Gemini's
  Uzbek polishes.
