## Reading these results

- PlagIQ finds **unchanged runs of seven or more words**. Copying that keeps such runs (unchanged, reformatted, script-switched, look-alike letters, lightly edited, reordered sentences) should be found; heavy editing that breaks most runs is expected to be missed. These numbers show how much.
- The cases are built from real texts, but the edits are simulated. Real student copying mixes them.
- Results depend on the library: run this script on the engine after imports to measure the live library.

## What this run found and what was changed

1. **Boilerplate caused most false marks.** Journal templates (the inscience.uz header "Жамият ва инновациялар – Society and innovations… Journal home page…", the "ARTICLE INFO / Article history" block and the licence line) and a CyberLeninka website box ("Не можете найти то, что вам нужно? Попробуйте сервис подбора литературы") matched unrelated articles. Two fixes:
   - a seven-word sequence found in **3 or more different texts** is treated as boilerplate and not counted (setting `BOILERPLATE_SOURCES`, default 3, 0 = off; applies to the private library and to texts fetched during a check);
   - the CyberLeninka box is removed on import.
2. **Effect** (same cases, filter off → on): false marks per 1,000 words in text that was not copied fell from 53.4 to 11.6 (English) and 14.1 to 0.8 (Russian); Uzbek 25.1 to 23.1 (see 3). Copied-text recall changed by at most 3 points. Whole held-out articles, highest match: English 9.22% → 2.15%, Russian 9.56% → 1.38%.
3. **The remaining Uzbek matches are real text reuse.** The "Oʻzbek adabiyoti" Wikipedia article repeats writers' biographies word for word from the individual articles (for example "1929 yil 1 martda Qoʻqon yaqinidagi Oxunqaynar qishlogʻida tugʻildi…"), so held-out articles about those writers match it. That is correct behaviour; in academic work such overlap is what a supervisor should see.
4. **Tables of numbers are not matched** (deliberately, since chart axes and year rows coincide across unrelated papers). The first run counted three table passages as missed copying; passages are now sampled from prose.
5. **What PlagIQ does not catch:** heavy editing (one word in four changed) is found in 0–2% of cases. That is the designed limit of exact seven-word matching; detecting it needs paraphrase methods, which PlagIQ does not claim.
